---
title: "Structure of the Finnish overtone flute"
---

<p style="font-size: 1.05rem; line-height: 1.6; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">
Overtone flutes can be divided into two different categories: block flutes and rim-blown flutes. These two types differ with each other how the air travels to the window. Flutes can also be divided by the location of the blowing hole: side-blown and end-blown flutes.
</p>

<p style="font-size: 1.05rem; line-height: 1.6; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">
Majority of the overtone flutes built in Finland nowadays are side-blown block flutes. Used materials have traditionally been bark, pipe plants or bone. At the present day most of the flutes are built of plastic tube, wood or metals.
</p>

<div class="tc pv2" style="margin-bottom: 2rem;">
  <img src="/overtoneflute-hugo/images/paapuhalteinen.jpg" alt="End-blown flute" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto; border-radius: 2px;">
  <p style="font-size: 0.9rem; color: #a0aec0; margin-top: 0.5rem; font-style: italic;">End-blown flute (builder: Max Brumberg)</p>
</div>

<p style="font-size: 1.05rem; line-height: 1.6; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">
Building a Finnish overtone flute is quite simple. The length of the tube affects the tuning and thickness to timbre and loudness. The building material affects e.g. to timbre and playing touch.
</p>

<p style="font-size: 1.05rem; line-height: 1.6; margin-bottom: 1.5rem;">
Nowadays it is common to use PVC plastic as a building material of overtone flutes. Blowing side's block could be made of wood or plastic. The flute's moisture tolerance will be improved if a plastic piece is put inside the tube as an extension for the wooden block. Plastic blocks won't swell because of the moisture and block entirely the air channel.
</p>

<p style="font-size: 1.05rem; line-height: 1.6; margin-bottom: 2rem;">
Also wooden season materials are handy, though short-lived. Willow species are good materials for overtone flute (willow flute) because the bark doesn't break easily when detached.
</p>
